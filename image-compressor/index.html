Image compressor and WebP converter

Drop in one or more images, pick WebP or JPEG and a quality, and download smaller files. Your images never leave your device.

Runs entirely in your browser. Nothing you type, paste or open here is uploaded to any server.

How do I compress an image for the web?

Resize it to the largest width it will be shown at, then save it as WebP at around 75 to 85 quality. Drop the image here, keep the defaults, and download the smaller file.

  1. Drop images onto the box or click to choose them.
  2. Pick WebP for most web use, JPEG for older systems, or PNG for lossless graphics.
  3. Set quality; 80 is a good start.
  4. Set a max width, for example 1600 for full-width images or 800 for blog inline images.
  5. Compare the before and after, then download.

Frequently asked questions

Are my images uploaded?

No. The browser's canvas does the resizing and encoding on your device.

Why did my PNG get bigger?

PNG is lossless, so re-saving a photo as PNG can grow it. Use WebP or JPEG for photos and keep PNG for logos and screenshots with flat colors.

Does it keep EXIF data like location?

No. Re-encoding through a canvas drops EXIF metadata, including GPS location, which is usually what you want for web images.

Do all browsers support WebP?

All current major browsers display WebP. Saving to WebP depends on your browser's encoder; if it is missing, the tool tells you and you can pick JPEG.
